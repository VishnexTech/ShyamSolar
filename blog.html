Planning

Is your roof ready for solar?

Roof space is only one part of the picture. Shade, structure, direction, and access all deserve a closer look.

Read article

A useful first assessment considers how much unshaded roof is available, how the roof faces, and whether nearby trees or structures cast shadows through the day. The roof's condition and safe access matter too. These details help a professional estimate a suitable layout; a photo alone cannot confirm structural capacity. Gather recent electricity bills and note any future changes to your home before a site visit.

Energy use

How to read your electricity bill

Your bill helps describe how much energy you use and when you use it.

Read article

Look for the billing period, total units consumed, sanctioned load, and any time-based charges. Compare several months rather than relying on a single unusually high or low bill. Your pattern of daytime use can affect how much solar energy you use directly. Keep bills available when requesting an estimate, and ask the installer to explain every assumption used in the proposal.

Installation

What happens during a solar installation?

A clear process starts with a site review and ends with checks, approvals, and a handover.

Read article

Most projects begin with an energy discussion and site assessment. The design is then reviewed alongside equipment choices, structural considerations, and local permissions. After approvals, installation is scheduled, electrical connections are checked, and any required utility inspection or meter process is completed. Ask who coordinates each stage, what access is needed, and what documents you will receive at handover.

System types

On-grid, off-grid, or hybrid?

System types work differently, especially when the grid is unavailable or batteries are involved.

Read article

An on-grid system works alongside the utility supply and may export surplus energy under applicable local rules. An off-grid setup operates independently and needs a plan for storage and backup generation. A hybrid design combines grid connection with battery capability. The right arrangement depends on local regulations, outage patterns, budget, and how much backup power you need. Confirm the details for your area before choosing.

Maintenance

Keeping solar panels clean

Dust and debris can affect output, but cleaning should always put safety first.

Read article

Follow the panel manufacturer's care instructions and use a qualified service provider when roof access is risky. Avoid abrasive tools, harsh chemicals, and cleaning hot panels with cold water. Check whether nearby trees, construction dust, or bird activity are creating recurring buildup. Monitoring system output over time can help distinguish a cleaning need from an electrical or equipment issue.

Business solar

Solar for a commercial facility

Business energy profiles, operating hours, and roof conditions shape the project design.

Read article

Start by reviewing interval usage data if available, monthly bills, and the facility's typical operating schedule. Check roof ownership, remaining roof life, access requirements, and any planned expansion. A proposal should explain system assumptions, expected production methodology, maintenance responsibilities, and how the project connects to your electrical infrastructure. Compare proposals on scope and service as well as price.

Utility guidance

Net metering: questions to ask

Export rules and meter processes depend on your local utility and current regulations.

Read article

Ask which connection category applies to your property, what documents are needed, whether a technical feasibility check is required, and how exported units are credited. Timelines and policies can change, so verify details with the local distribution company or regulator. Your installer should identify which party handles each application step and whether any charges or capacity limits apply.

Equipment

Choosing the right solar inverter

The inverter converts panel output into usable electricity and plays a key role in monitoring.

Read article

Compare inverter type, suitable system size, monitoring features, service coverage, and warranty terms. Ask how the equipment handles shade and whether panel-level electronics may be appropriate for your roof. Confirm where the inverter will be installed and how it can be accessed for service. Equipment selection should follow the system design rather than being chosen in isolation.

Energy storage

When might a home battery help?

A battery can provide backup or shift energy use, but it adds cost and design decisions.

Read article

Start with the loads you want to keep running during an outage, how long they need power, and how often outages occur. Battery capacity, inverter compatibility, charging strategy, space, and replacement expectations all matter. A battery is not automatically needed for every grid-connected system. Compare the additional cost and expected use with your household's priorities before deciding.

Weather

Solar panels during the monsoon

Rainfall and cloud cover change solar production, so seasonal expectations matter.

Read article

Solar panels continue producing in cloudy conditions, though output is lower when sunlight is reduced. A yearly estimate should reflect seasonal weather patterns rather than assuming the same production every month. Check that mounting, wiring, drainage, and roof penetrations are suitable for local conditions. After severe weather, arrange an inspection if you see damage or a sudden change in system performance.

Choosing an installer

Questions to ask before you sign

A good proposal should be clear about the design, equipment, scope, service, and exclusions.

Read article

Ask what site measurements were used, which products are proposed, who performs the installation, and what warranties apply to equipment and workmanship. Request a breakdown of inclusions, exclusions, timelines, payment milestones, approvals, and after-install support. Confirm that projected production and savings are estimates with stated assumptions, not guarantees. Keep a copy of the final design and commissioning records.

Warranties

Understanding solar warranties

Panel, inverter, battery, and workmanship coverage are different things; read each separately.

Read article

Product warranties cover specified equipment issues under stated conditions, while performance warranties describe a minimum output over time. Installation workmanship coverage is a separate promise from the installer. Check who handles claims, what proof is required, whether transport or labour is included, and which exclusions apply. Keep invoices, serial numbers, system records, and warranty documents together for future reference.
